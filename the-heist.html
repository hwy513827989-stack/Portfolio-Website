Overview

Producing a 3D Action Short in Six Weeks

The Heist is a wordless animated short developed by a five-person team. Set in a stylised desert environment, the film follows two animal criminals fleeing the police after a bank robbery, ending in a shootout at an abandoned roadside diner.

I handled most of the film's 3D production, including character rigging, UV unwrapping, texturing, environment production, keyframe animation, camera work, lighting, and Arnold rendering. I also drew the storyboard, produced the animatic, and assembled the rough cut.

STORYBOARD & ANIMATIC

Planning the Chase

I drew the storyboard and edited it into an animatic to establish shot order, pacing, screen direction, and the transition from the highway chase to the diner sequence.

CHARACTER PRODUCTION

Rigging, UVs, and Deformation

I built the final police model and completed the UV unwrapping, texturing, materials, rigging, skinning, manual weight painting, and limited facial blend shapes for all three characters.

The rabbit and raccoon base models were created by another project contributor.

ENVIRONMENT, ANIMATION & LIGHTING

Building and Staging the Desert World

The film was animated through hand-keyed poses and camera movement. I built the desert terrain and major buildings, staged the environments, lit the full sequence, and rendered the shots using Arnold Standard Surface materials and Arnold Renderer.

Reflection

What I Would Change

REFLECTION 01

Prioritising Shot Quality

The large shot count created uneven polish across the final film. In a future production, I would reduce the number of shots and reserve more time for animation timing, deformation cleanup, and continuity between cameras.

REFLECTION 02

Designing Around Rig Limitations

Manual weighting improved the main joint deformations, but some extreme poses still created intersections. I adjusted poses and camera placement to reduce visible clipping. A future rig would need more corrective blend shapes and earlier deformation testing.
